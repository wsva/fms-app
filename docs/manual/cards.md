# Cards

Cards is FmS's flashcard system, built on **spaced repetition**: the app schedules each card to reappear just as you're about to forget it, so you remember more words in less time. Cards live in **Card** datasets and can be created by hand, generated from your reading, or synced from others.

## The three views

Open **Cards** from the sidebar and pick a card dataset at the top. The toolbar switches between three views, and the breadcrumb's **Cards** crumb returns you to the main list from either of the others:

- **Cards** — browse and manage the deck (the default view).
- **Review** — sit down and review the cards that are due.
- **Tags** — organise and filter cards by tag.

The toolbar **Refresh** re‑reads the current list; the overflow menu opens the **locations** manager to add card‑dataset folders.

## Reviewing cards

Review is where spaced repetition happens:

1. You see a card's front, think of the answer, then **flip** it to reveal the back.
2. You rate how well you recalled it using four buttons — from "again" (got it wrong) through "hard", "good", to "easy". A **skip** option steps past a card without grading it.
3. Based on your rating, the app reschedules the card: easy cards come back later, difficult ones return sooner. This is the SM‑2 style algorithm that spreads reviews out over days, weeks, and months.

Reviewing is how vocabulary moves from short‑term to long‑term memory, so do it regularly even outside of practice sessions.

## Creating and editing cards

You can add cards directly (the **Add/Plus** action) or edit an existing one in the card editor. Because the editor is shared across the app, you can also:

- Create a card from a word or phrase while reading — see [Books](books.md).
- Capture a tricky dictation cue as a card — see [Dictation](dictation.md).

A card generally pairs a **question** (the prompt) with an **answer**, and may carry extra fields and tags.

## Tags

Tags let you group cards — by topic, source, difficulty, or anything you like. Use the **Tags** view to manage them, and tag cards so you can filter and study focused subsets.

## Search

The Cards view has a unified **search box**. A scope toggle switches between searching **this dataset** and searching **all your card datasets** at once, using full‑text search so results match words inside card content, not just titles.

## Known simple words

**Tools → Simple Words** is a companion to Cards. It lets you mark words you already know (drawn from your selected card datasets, per language) so features like word generation and dictation can avoid re‑testing vocabulary you've mastered. See [Tools](tools.md).

## Multiple datasets and locations

Card datasets each hold their own deck, tags, and review schedule. You can have many — one per book, per language, or per theme — and add folders of them through the locations manager. Review state (which cards are due) is remembered per card, so your schedule survives switching between decks.
