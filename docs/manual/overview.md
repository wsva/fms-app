# Overview

FmS turns authentic language material — podcasts, audiobooks, graded readers, sentence collections — into structured practice. Instead of a fixed curriculum, you bring (or download) **datasets** and the app helps you work through them in four complementary ways.

## The four practice modes

| Mode | What you do | Best for |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Dictation** | Listen to a short audio cue and type exactly what you hear; the app checks you against the transcript. | Listening accuracy, spelling, hearing connected speech. |
| **Read a Book** | Read a text split into chapters and sentences, look up words, and generate vocabulary. | Comprehension and vocabulary in context. |
| **Read Aloud** | Record yourself reading a passage; speech‑to‑text scores how closely you matched the text. | Pronunciation and fluency. |
| **Cards** | Review flashcards with spaced repetition (a proven "remember longer with less review" schedule). | Long‑term vocabulary retention. |

There is also a **Wiki** area for Markdown document collections you can read, edit, search, and keep in sync — useful for notes, grammar references, or bilingual texts.

## Datasets are the shared foundation

Every practice mode reads from a dataset. A dataset is a normal folder on your computer containing media, text, and generated helper files (subtitles, waveforms, a small database). The same dataset can drive dictation, reading, cards, and more, so your material stays consistent across modes. See [Datasets & the Processing Pipeline](datasets.md) for the details.

Datasets come in five types — **Dictation, Cards, Books, Read Aloud, Wiki** — and each is shown under its own tab wherever datasets are listed.

## Three ways to run FmS

- **Desktop app** — the full experience: import datasets from disk, download and run speech‑to‑text models locally, process pipelines, OCR, and all tools.
- **Android app** — a "thin client" that focuses on practice and sync. It is designed to follow the datasets and progress you keep on a computer rather than being the place where heavy processing happens.
- **Website** — a browser version with an account system, an online dataset **Market**, a blog, and most practice modes. It is also the natural **Hub** for sharing datasets with your other devices.

The app and website are the same product concept; the website adds hosted features (accounts, market, blog) while the desktop app adds local power (running models offline, file access, heavy processing).

## Learning together across devices

FmS can keep your datasets and progress in step between a computer and your phones. One workspace acts as the **Hub** (the authoritative store everyone syncs against); the others are **Followers**. This is fully covered in [Using FmS on Multiple Devices](multiple_devices.md).

## Progress and motivation

As you complete cues, sentences, and recordings you earn **XP** and rise through **levels**, shown next to your name in the sidebar. XP is permanent and can't be bought or traded — it simply records how much you've practiced. Dictation and reading earn XP per completed cue/sentence, with bonuses for finishing a whole subtitle, media file, or chapter.

## Built to be automated

Every action you can take in the interface is also exposed through a built‑in **MCP server**, so an AI agent (or a script) can import datasets, generate subtitles, run reviews, and manage sync on your behalf. You don't need to touch this to use FmS, but it means the app is safe to hand to an assistant for repetitive work. See the **Agent‑friendly design** note in [Reference](reference.md).
